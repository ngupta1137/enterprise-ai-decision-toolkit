# Field Data Foundations: A Checklist for Ag Robotics Startups

Your robot will gather more data in one season than your company will know what to do with in five years. Whether that data becomes an asset or a liability is decided at the start, by foundations, not later by tools. Work through this list before your first full season in the field.

## 1. Know what you are collecting, and why

- [ ] Every sensor and stream is listed, with the decision it is meant to improve.
- [ ] Anything collected "because we might need it" is named as such, and kept on purpose or not collected.
- [ ] Each stream has an owner on the team, by name.

## 2. The grower agreement comes first

Your field data exists because a grower let you onto their land. That relationship is the foundation.

- [ ] A written agreement with each grower partner covers what you record, where it goes, and who owns it.
- [ ] The agreement says what happens to the data if the partnership ends.
- [ ] Faces, workers, and license plates: you have a rule for incidental capture of people, and you follow it.
- [ ] The grower can see what you hold about their farm, on request.

## 3. Capture it clean, or do not capture it

- [ ] Every record carries time, place, and conditions (weather, crop stage, machine settings). A reading without context cannot be trusted later.
- [ ] Units, formats, and naming are fixed in one written standard the whole team uses.
- [ ] Failed runs and bad data are kept and marked, not quietly deleted. Your future model is only as honest as your worst records.
- [ ] Someone checks data quality weekly during the season, not at the end of it.

## 4. Store it like you will be asked about it

Because you will be, by investors, by enterprise customers, and by regulators.

- [ ] One known place where the data lives, with access limited to named people.
- [ ] Backups exist and have been tested once.
- [ ] You can answer, in one page, what you hold, where it came from, and what you have shared with anyone.
- [ ] Retention is a decision, not an accident: what you keep, for how long, and what you delete.

## 5. Build the asset, honestly

- [ ] Labels and ground truth are recorded with who made them and when, so a model trained on them can be defended.
- [ ] Train and test data are kept separate from the day you start, not reconstructed later.
- [ ] If a model or a partner's tool touches your data, the terms are written down before it happens.
- [ ] You can show an investor or a customer the line from raw field data to the claim in your pitch deck. If you cannot, the claim comes out of the deck.
